Three steps, every time

Take off any magnetic case — MagSafe magnets are far stronger than anything you are looking for. Hold the phone away from metal and cables and tap Calibrate; after about two seconds the reading settles near zero. Then move slowly: the number shows how much the field has changed since you calibrated, and the spot where it peaks is where the metal is.

Wire mode does not react to a cable I know is live

Wire mode looks for the magnetic signature of current that is actually flowing, not for the presence of a wire. A cable feeding an LED lamp, a phone charger or a switched-off appliance draws far too little to detect. Switch on something power-hungry on that circuit — a hair dryer, heater or kettle — then rest the phone still against the spot for about three seconds. It works on both 50 Hz and 60 Hz mains, with nothing to set.

Where is the sensor in my phone?

Near the top, around the camera area, though the exact spot varies between models. To find yours: switch to Metal, calibrate, then slide a screw slowly across the back of the phone. Wherever the number jumps most is your sensor — lead with that edge when you sweep.

How do I find a wooden stud?

You find the screws that hold it. Put the phone flat on the wall in Stud mode and slide it sideways. A peak is a screw. Mark it, then check 30–40 cm above and below: several peaks in a vertical line mean you found the stud.

Can it find gold, copper or plastic pipe? How deep is it?

No, and no phone app can. A magnetometer senses ferromagnetic metal — iron and steel — and the changing field of live wiring. Gold, silver, copper, aluminum, brass and plastic are invisible to it. Depth cannot be measured either: a strong reading may be a small screw close by or a large mass further in. For dead cables and non-magnetic targets, a capacitive stud finder or a wall-scanning radar is the right instrument.

The reading never sits at exactly zero

A drift of a fraction of a microtesla is normal — the sensor is that sensitive. If it sits many microtesla above zero, something magnetic is close by: a case, a laptop, a speaker, a fridge door. Move away and calibrate again.

Ferrum collects no data. The app has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code. It never connects to the internet: every measurement happens on your device, and the only stored values (your mode, sensitivity, units and sound settings) stay in your device's local storage. Nothing is ever transmitted to us or to anyone else.
